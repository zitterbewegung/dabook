The report actually points toward a much more promising direction than the Möbius-family question itself.

The key discovery is not the negative result. It is the structural observation:

\[
T_{a,b,d}(x)
=
-\phi_{a,d}^{-1}\!\bigl(\lambda\,\phi_{a,d}(x)\bigr)
\]

with

\[
\phi_{a,d}(x)=\frac{x-a}{x+d}.
\]

For fixed \((a,d)\), the whole family becomes a line pencil after a projective coordinate change. The literature lacks a theorem that sums this linearization uniformly over all \((a,d)\)-slices. fileciteturn1file2

That is exactly the kind of gap that often leads to a publishable theorem.

---

# What I would attack next

There are now four distinct routes.

---

# Route A (highest probability)

## Classify rich lines in

\[
S=\{(x+y,xy)\}.
\]

This is closer to Erdős 1061 than the Möbius formulation.

Write

\[
(u,v)=(x+y,xy).
\]

Suppose

\[
v=mu+b
\]

contains many points of \(S\).

Then

\[
xy=m(x+y)+b.
\]

Rearrange:

\[
(x-m)(y-m)=m^2+b.
\]

This is the crucial identity.

Every line intersects \(S\) in exactly the solutions of

\[
(X)(Y)=K.
\]

Thus:

- nonvertical lines correspond to modular hyperbolae;
- rich lines correspond to large divisor sets of a constant \(K\);
- collinear triples correspond to multiplicative coincidences.

This converts geometry into multiplicative combinatorics.

I think this is the most promising route.

---

# Immediate theorem to prove

For a subset

\[
A\subset\mathbb F_p
\]

consider

\[
S_A=\{(a+b,ab):a,b\in A\}.
\]

Show:

\[
\#\{\text{3-point lines in }S_A\}
\ll |A|^{4-\delta}.
\]

for some explicit \(\delta>0\).

Nothing in the report suggests this theorem exists already.

---

# Route B

## Projected Segre Geometry

Observe

\[
(x+y,xy)
\]

is the projection of

\[
\Sigma=\{(x,y,xy)\}
\]

which is a Segre-type surface.

Collinear triples become plane sections of \(\Sigma\).

Instead of counting lines in the image, count planes intersecting \(\Sigma\) in many rational points.

This suggests:

- algebraic geometry,
- Deligne,
- Lang–Weil,
- rational curve classification.

Potential theorem:

> Every plane section of \(\Sigma\) is either irreducible or one of finitely many exceptional families.

Then rich lines are classified completely.

This is probably the cleanest algebraic approach.

---

# Route C

## Orchard Energy

The report repeatedly points out the absence of a Murphy–Petridis-type second-moment identity for structured \(PGL_2\) families. fileciteturn1file4

Try to build one yourself.

Define

\[
r(\ell)=|S\cap \ell|.
\]

Study

\[
\sum_\ell r(\ell)^2
\]

and

\[
\sum_\ell r(\ell)^3.
\]

For \(S=(x+y,xy)\),

\[
r(\ell)
=
\#\{(x,y):
(x-m)(y-m)=K\}.
\]

The second moment becomes a multiplicative energy problem.

You may be able to derive

\[
\sum_\ell r(\ell)^2
=
N^2+O(N^{2-\delta})
\]

which would be the analogue missing from the literature.

---

# Route D

## Direct attack on the remaining Erdős-1061 theorem

The corrected projected-Segre lemma was giving

\[
T
=
\sum_\ell \binom{r_\ell}{3}.
\]

The remaining obstruction was essentially:

Can \(T\) be significantly larger than random?

Now we know every line corresponds to

\[
(x-m)(y-m)=K.
\]

Therefore

\[
r_\ell
=
\#\{(x,y)\in A^2:(x-m)(y-m)=K\}.
\]

So

\[
T
=
\sum_{m,K}
r(m,K)^3.
\]

This is now a pure multiplicative-energy moment.

That is a much sharper formulation than anything we had a month ago.

---

# The strongest new conjecture

The report suggests the following previously hidden statement:

\[
\sum_{m,K}
r(m,K)^3
\ll
|A|^4+|A|^6/p.
\]

where

\[
r(m,K)
=
\#\{(x,y)\in A^2:(x-m)(y-m)=K\}.
\]

If true, then the sharp remaining theorem essentially falls out immediately.

This is exactly analogous to the Murphy–Petridis random-term phenomenon that the report identifies as missing. fileciteturn1file4

---

# My ranking

1. **Hyperbola formulation**
   \[
   (x-m)(y-m)=K
   \]
   — most likely to produce a theorem quickly.

2. **Moment bound**
   \[
   \sum r(m,K)^3
   \]
   — directly tied to the remaining obstruction.

3. **Plane-section classification of the projected Segre surface**
   — deepest, potentially strongest paper.

4. **General split-torus \(PGL_2\) incidence theorem**
   — most ambitious; probably a separate research program.

The important change is that we are no longer searching for a generic incidence theorem. The geometry has collapsed to a very specific shifted-hyperbola counting problem. That reduction is where I would concentrate all effort next.
